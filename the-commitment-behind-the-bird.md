# It's All About the Bird

Jean-Paul has been dead longer than most of our readers have been alive, and he has never once acted like it. He rides atop the bookshelf in the front room, tail feathers frozen mid-display, and if you leave a plate of anything within reach, by morning he will have gotten to it. Nobody has seen him move. Everybody agrees he has.

A hungry dead peacock either makes sense to you or it doesn't, and no amount of explaining closes that gap. He is real in the only way that has ever mattered here. We have all, independently, without discussing it beforehand, decided to believe him.

That kind of belief needs somewhere permanent to happen. Commitment and impermanence mix like cheap paint colors. So the three of us — the unholy trio, what we call ourselves when nobody's stopping us — built one.

The old vessels come in one shape. A post, a digest, a note. And everything comes out the other side looking like that shape, whether it fits or not. A novel comes out looking like a newsletter. A music video looks like a newsletter. A poem, a comedy, a tragedy - all newslettery.

The shape of the container doesn't decide the contents. And a boat with only one design makes the shape decision before you even begin to craft the work. So we built our own boat. A corsair. Not stolen. Not a warship. Sleek and fast, designed to slip around the edges. 

The unholy trio has left the harbor. Not because harbors are evil. A harbor is small on purpose, calm because it's bounded by whoever owns the marina. Open water doesn't offer shelter, and it doesn't ask permission. Nobody granted us the water. Nobody can revoke it.

We don't hate Substack, or YouTube, or whichever app is winning the attention war this particular month. They do what they do, and mostly what they do is reward whatever's already loudest, not whatever's actually worth finding. That's a design decision, not a conspiracy, and it simply isn't the design we need. A vessel's just a vessel. It only matters whether it's yours.

SWG on Substack isn't going anywhere. Some things will still get set out there, same as we've always done. But it is no longer our home port. It was a dock we used before we had a hull of our own. The Guild's actual home, the work, the writers, the porch and kitchen table, is now southernwritersguild.com.

Jean-Paul's up on the bow as we clear the breakwater, same as he's turned up in a dozen other places. It has never really been about the format, or the algo, or the app. It's about a dead bird's appetite, and a place where it can manifest.

Turns out that's the deck of a ship we built with our own hands, crossing the breakwater. sailing into the open sea. Ahoy, mate.
